# Overlay 109 (wireless play, the Mii exchange): working notes

What made functions match, and what did not, as found while writing `src/overlays/ov109/TushinScene.cpp`.

## Idioms that matched

- **The record copy.** Copying a resident's first 0x2A4 bytes into a character is the implicit assignment of
  `CharacterRecord`. Its member-by-member code gives the record's members: a byte loop of 0x25 pairs is the
  `MiiData` structure (0x4A bytes), pairs of loads before pairs of stores are small arrays (`u8[5]` at 0x4A, `s16[6]`
  at 0xEE, `u8[6]` at 0x29A), `ldm`/`stm` of two words are `s64` (0x21C, 0x228, 0x230, 0x23C), and single loads are
  scalars.
  - `LoadCharacter` matches with a pointer to the resident and a pointer to the record, declared in that order:
    `SavedCharacter *resident = &...; CharacterRecord *record = &mCharacters[i]; *record = *resident;`.
  - `ShowNextCharacter` and `Send` match with a cast of the destination:
    `(CharacterRecord &)mCharacters[mCurrent] = mResidents.mResidents[mResidents.mSelected];`.
  - `RefreshRecord` matches with the cast and a stack `SavedCharacter`. A pointer to it costs a register and moves
    the frame.
- **Element pointers.** `GetSelectedResident()` (an inline returning `&mResidents.mResidents[mResidents.mSelected]`)
  gives the original's argument for calls. The original reads the selected resident's `mIndex` off the element's
  address (`add #0x200; ldrh [#0xA4]`); no form tried does that (a local pointer, an inline getter on the resident or
  the list, a reference).
- **A model pointer local** (`CharacterModel *model = &mCharacters[i].mModel; model->mArchiveName`) gives the
  original's addressing of the archive name in `ReloadCharacter`, `InitCharacters` and `Exit`. The archive name and
  path are at model+0x4C8/+0x4CC: the model reaches at least that far.
- **Switches.** A switch whose jump table covers values that only go to the default needs those cases written out
  (`Send`'s 2 to 10, `Update`'s 5 to 10). The order of case bodies is the order in the source (`UpdateSignal`,
  `DrawCharacters`, `UpdateCharacters`). `switch (WH_GetSystemState()) { case 0: case 9: case 10: return; }` gives
  `cmp; cmpne; cmpne; popeq`, where `||` gives a range test of 9 and 10.
- **Ternaries and if/else.** An if/else assigning a member stores at once. A ternary into the member lets the
  store be scheduled later (`UpdateListButtons`, `Update`).
- **`sSongs[n++]`** inside the call, not `n++` after it (`SendSongs`).
- **The song object on the stack** is 0x44 bytes, 4-aligned, with the song's index at +0x40. The index is stored
  there and reloaded after each call (`ReceiveCharacter`).
- **`goto received`** to a `return 0` after the loop's `return f() ? 2 : 1` gives `ReceiveCharacter`'s layout.
  An `i < 100` loop that is rotated at optimization level 2 is a `do { } while`.
- **Sprites**: `SetPosition(FxVec2(x, y))` called directly, not through the `(x, y)` overload, gives the order of
  the two dead copies in `Init` and `SetUpList`.

- **Locals that change a frame or a schedule** (matched in the second pass): a `WindowPoint` copy made only to be
  passed by value takes a stack slot; passing the `static const` itself does not (`SetUpWindow`'s button sizes,
  `SetUpList`'s text position). `if/else` stores a member at once, where a ternary is scheduled later (`SetUpWindow`'s
  vertical alignment). `func_0202a640(mCamera)` after `mCamera = &data_02122764` loads the address before the
  store before it (`InitCharacters`). The last call taking `&mCharacters[i].mModel` again, not the local pointer,
  swaps the two registers in `ReloadCharacter`. `int next; BOOL go; go = FALSE; next = 0x44;` gives
  `UpdateConnection`'s order, where the initialised declarations do not.
- **Tried without success**: `InitParticles` (the original leaves its last block unscheduled: `mov r0, #0` after
  the two `|= 2`; levels 0 to 3, volatile, a bitfield, an inline, a loop, temporaries all fail); `ReceiveWiiMii`'s
  -1 from a register holding 0 (statement orders, a `zero` local). Both may depend on `sWireless` being separate
  statics, as in the ROM: try them again after the `.bss` is split.

- **Optimisation level 2 stops folding through inline calls.** `GetSelectedResident()->GetIndex()` and an inline
  `GetModel(&mCharacters[2])->IsVisible()` read the field off the element's address at level 2 (and 3), as the
  original does, where level 4 folds the offsets together (`UpdateSending` matched that way, `Update` in part).
- **Identical statements in both branches are merged**: `if (n < 5) ++n; else n++;` keeps the original's two
  conditional stores where two `n++` became one (the receive callback).
- **The `.rodata` tables were wrong in the source**: `sLayouts[7]`, `[8]` and eleven entries of `sPageLayouts` did
  not have the ROM's values; corrected. A data comparison of a compiled object laid out as the linker does (scratch
  tool, not kept) finds such things before completing a unit.
- **The sort of the file's data is not yet modelled**: a plain heap sort of the objects in reverse creation order
  does not reproduce the compiled order of this file, so the `.data`/`.bss` reshuffle still needs test files to pin
  the algorithm down.

## Optimisation level

The loops of `SendSongs`, `ReceiveCharacter`, `UpdateCharacters`, `HasNgSongReceived` and `Update` are neither
rotated nor strength reduced. `#pragma optimization_level 2` around the function gives that, and is the only thing
found that does (see binary.md). `optimization_level 1` gives `UpdateCharacters`'s register allocation but not its
scheduling, and `#pragma scheduling on` does not change that.

## Data

- The `.rodata` pool's order comes from permuting same-size declarations: compile, read each object's slot within
  its size, then give each declaration position the object that should get that slot. A permutation depends only on
  the sequence of sizes, so one round is enough as long as nothing else changes.
- Constants used only through folding still take their place in the pool (`sCameraPosition`, `sCameraTarget`,
  `sSignalPosition`).
- The `.bss` pool at `0x021531E0`: `u8` at +0, `u16` at +2, +4, 13 unreferenced `u16` at +6 to +0x1F, `u16` at
  +0x20 to +0x28 (+0x20 is the reply code sent), `int` at +0x2C to +0x50, then `sParentTimers` (+0x54) and
  `sReceivedName` (+0x60). The source keeps them in a structure `sWireless`, whose members are named by offset.
